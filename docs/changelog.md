- 2026-11-17 — **The two Qwen3-4B encoders are one model, and the operator kept both.** The question was whether FLUX.2's 8.05 GB bfloat16 encoder could be replaced by the 4.41 GB int8 copy Z-Image already ships, which would have turned 95.8 s of CPU encode into 2.5 s on the card. It can: the three tokenizer files are **byte-identical**, `embed_tokens` and `model.norm` agree bit for bit, all thirty-six layers correlate at **1.0000** and the 0.0039 maximum difference on quantised tensors is int8's own error. Two facts came out of the comparison that were not the point of it. **Z-Image's int8 copy carries a structurally pruned last layer** — 5770 of 9728 rows of `layers.35.mlp.up_proj` and 404 of 2560 of `layers.35.mlp.down_proj` hold a zero scale, the only two of 252 quantised tensors, while their non-zero rows match the bfloat16 weights to the last digit — and it is invisible to FLUX.2, which conditions on the hidden states of layers 9, 18 and 27 and never reaches layer 35. **And the RAM does not fall**: `requantize` materialises the model on the host before moving it, measured at 5.05 GB peak resident as written, **5.29 GB** with a lazy state dict that never builds the dictionary, and an out-of-memory failure when the empty shell is placed on the card first — the encoder is 4.41 GB and it has to live somewhere, host or device. The reuse measured **2.5 s against 95.8 s** at a cosine of 0.99998 per layer, but five to ten per cent relative deviation accumulated over 27 layers of float32 activations, and whether that reaches a still was not judged. **Ruled on 2026-11-17: the official bfloat16 encoder stays.** What would reopen it is a blind comparison of the two encoders' stills at one prompt and seed.
