- 2026-11-19 — **The turbo LoRA buys its speed by dropping the negative branch, and the operator's eye caught the cost.** `docs/research/p10-b3-dreamshaper-and-moss-stage2-report.md` §17 and ledger §20.7 record it. The operator insisted his CFG 3,0 image has better detail, colouration, style, bottles and **skin that does not look gummy**. **The log proves the mechanism:** CFG 3,0 runs **two conditioning passes** — positive and the negative prompt `(worst quality, low quality, illustration, 3d, 2d, painting, cartoons, sketch)` — while **CFG 1,0 runs one and never encodes the negative at all**. So **the turbo's speed does not come from the LoRA; it comes from removing guidance**, and with the negative branch gone the model's default tendency toward plastic surfaces is unopposed, which is exactly the sheen on forehead and cheekbone that he described. **What is not saturation:** global saturation and colorfulness are nearly identical between his pick and the turbo (61,4 % / 54,2 against 61,3 % / 55,3), and the no-LoRA 12-step is actually the most saturated (63,4 % / 58,1). What his image has is **object and material variety** — bottles of different silhouettes and glasses in amber, green and clear, where the turbo drifts to uniform elongated bottles with pale labels — and **matte versus sheen**. **Verdict corrected:** §16 called the turbo "better hands", which holds on that crop, but **weighting it as the main criterion was a proportion error** — hands are one region, while skin, bottles, style and colour are the whole frame. **Quality configuration: CFG 3,0 without the LoRA**, and at 12 steps it is also the sharpest (952,4) with the **lowest peak of all** (6.394 MiB) at 52,2 s. **The turbo is a speed concession, not an improvement:** 40,4 s in exchange for losing the negative prompt.

- 2026-11-19 — **Sharpness is not quality: the operator's pick is the grainiest, not the best-detailed, and 12 steps without the LoRA is the best of the four.** `docs/research/p10-b3-dreamshaper-and-moss-stage2-report.md` §16 and ledger §20.6 record it. The operator pointed at the **no-LoRA 8-step CFG 3,0 render** (47,6 s, 5.994 MiB) as the one where quality shows, and the check on an identical crop splits the verdict. **Laplacian variance agrees with him:** 849,1 for his pick against 620,3 for the turbo at 8 steps, 710,3 for the turbo at 12 and **952,4 for no-LoRA at 12 steps** — but the metric measures **local high-frequency contrast**, and CFG 3,0 adds **grain** to felt and skin, which inflates the number without adding detail. **At 3,5× the order inverts:** his pick has readable anatomy but **waxy skin, soft fingertips, no nails and no knuckle creases**, while the **turbo at 8 steps shows knuckle creases and fingernails and has the best hands of the three**, with the no-LoRA 12-step close behind. **What he saw is real — photographic texture and grain, reading best at full size — but grain is not detail.** **Two defensible configurations:** for photographic texture and the lowest peak, **no LoRA at 12 steps, CFG 3,0 → 52,2 s at 6.394 MiB**, sharpest of the four and second-best hands; for the best hands and the fastest render, **turbo at 8 steps → 40,4 s at 6.686 MiB**. His instinct pointed at CFG 3,0 without the LoRA, and taken to 12 steps it is the best of the four on texture and peak. **Third method correction of the day: Laplacian variance rewards grain**, so it is used here to describe texture only and never to conclude anatomy, which is decided on the magnified crop.

- 2026-11-19 — **Correction: 12 steps is worth it, the turbo LoRA barely helps there, and the cache LoRA is deleted with no substitute worth hunting.** `docs/research/p10-b3-dreamshaper-and-moss-stage2-report.md` §15 and ledger §20.5 record it. **The cache LoRA is removed:** the blocker is the runtime, not the adapter — `sd.cpp` has no text K/V caching, so **any correction LoRA for that mechanism has nothing to correct**, and fetching another file would repeat the same mistake. **My 12-step dismissal was lazy and is withdrawn:** §12.2 compared time against time and never looked at the image; seen, the 12-step render is **better** — sharper faces and hands, crisper background bottles, σ 61,18 against 60,40. **The comparison that was missing:** DMD alone at 4 / 6 / 8 steps gives 34,3 / 37,8 / 47,6 s at 5.994 MiB; turbo at 8 steps gives **40,4 s** at 6.686 MiB; turbo at 12 steps gives **49,7 s** at 6.686 MiB with σ 61,18; and **no LoRA at 12 steps gives 52,2 s at 6.394 MiB with σ 63,24 — the lowest peak and the highest contrast of the three.** **The turbo at 12 steps saves only 2,5 s** while costing 292 MiB and some contrast, where at 8 steps it saved 7,2 s: **fixed cost dominates**, since loading the weights (~15 s) plus the Qwen3.5 encoder (~11 s) is ~26 s independent of step count. **The decision is therefore about steps, not about the LoRA:** best quality is 12 steps without the LoRA (52,2 s, 6.394 MiB), fastest is 8 steps with the turbo (40,4 s, 6.686 MiB), and 12 steps with turbo sits in between. Whether 9 s justify the visible jump from 8 to 12 steps is an operator call, not a measurement. Single runs under variable load, so the ordering is sound but 2–3 s deltas are not precise.

- 2026-11-19 — **KV-cache is closed on both routes: the LoRA has nothing to correct and the runtime's cache does not pay at 8 steps.** `docs/research/p10-b3-dreamshaper-and-moss-stage2-report.md` §13 and ledger §20.3 record it. **`p1atdev/MOSS-Image-SFT-KV-Cache-LoRA` is not applicable and was not downloaded:** its README calls it *"an experimental correction LoRA for fixed-timestep text KV caching"* and states **"the adapter alone does not enable caching"** — the mechanism lives in a ComfyUI node (`text_timestep` 1000) that computes text-only attention K/V once and reuses them, while `sd.cpp` does not implement that approximation, so the adapter would inject a correction for an error the runtime never makes. **The equivalent inside `sd.cpp` — `--cache-mode` with `dbcache`, `taylorseer`, `cache-dit` and `easycache` — was measured on the recommended configuration and does not pay:** no cache **40,4 s**, `dbcache` at threshold 0,25 with warmup 4 **41,1 s**, `cache-dit` **42,0 s**, and only the aggressive `dbcache` at threshold 0,5 with warmup 1 reaches **38,7 s** while **darkening and flattening the image** (mean luminance 49,22 → 40,42, sigma 60,40 → 50,58, mean difference 23,03/255).
